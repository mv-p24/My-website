#### **Bio:** 

I am a Manager in Climate Risk at EY's Climate Change and Sustainability Services practice in New York, where I co-lead the climate risk team. I advise Fortune 500 companies on physical and transition risk, decarbonization strategy, and climate data. My work connects scenario analysis to capital planning, asset valuation, and investment decisions.

I earned a PhD in Environmental Economics from the University of Miami's Rosenstiel School in 2024. My research estimates how climate hazards and resilience investments are priced into property values. It includes peer-reviewed economics research, a regional flood-risk study for the Tampa Bay Regional Planning Council, and a resilience policy toolkit for the University's Office of Civic Engagement. See [Projects](/project/) for details.

I also hold an M.S. in Natural Hazards Analysis and a B.S. in Mathematics and Economics from the University of Miami, and the GARP Sustainability and Climate Risk (SCR) certificate.

Outside of work, I enjoy golf and basketball.

#### **Experience:**

**Manager, Climate Risk** | EY, Climate Change and Sustainability Services (CCaSS) | New York | 2024–present

Co-lead EY's climate risk team, advising Fortune 500 companies on physical and transition risk, decarbonization strategy, and climate data. The work connects scenario analysis to the decisions it should inform: capital planning, asset valuation, and investment prioritization. Clients span consumer, healthcare, agriculture and financial services.

Key highlights:

- Customized GCAM-derived scenario analysis, to estimate sector and commodity-price sensitivities and flag input-cost volatility and margin pressure across multiple regions.
- Quantify physical climate risk across asset portfolios using natural-hazard and climate risk models, translating hazard exposure and business interruption into cash-flow and net-operating-income impacts.
- Advise clients on decarbonization strategy, weighing the cost and abatement potential of levers such as energy efficiency, renewable procurement, and RECs against energy-price forecasts to prioritize actions that cut emissions and operating cost together.
- Lead vendor management and data solutioning for the practice. Serve as the department's climate risk subject-matter expert for business development, owning pricing and technical content for RFPs, proposals, and scopes of work.
- Drive solution development and AI integration for the climate risk team, bringing AI into analysis and delivery across engagements.
- Directed development of portfolio-scale analytics that replaced manual screening with repeatable R and Python workflows, cutting engagement timelines by 30% and moving client teams from risk identification to investment prioritization.
- Deliver climate risk and scenario analysis reporting for 20+ companies under CSRD, TCFD, ISSB, CDP, California SB 261, and Australian ASRS.

**PhD Researcher** | University of Miami, Department of Environmental Science and Policy | Miami, FL | 2018–2024

Doctoral research in climate and environmental economics at the University of Miami, estimating how climate hazards and resilience investments are capitalized into property values. The work speaks directly to asset repricing, collateral risk, and insurance exposure in climate-exposed markets. The core skill is research design that supports causal inference, applied through difference-in-differences, hedonic pricing, fixed-effects panel, and spatial econometric models on multi-million-observation property panels.

Key highlights:

- Led execution of an econometric study on multi-gigabyte property data, published in a top field environmental economics journal, that produced causal evidence that climate-resilience investments (seawalls, water pumps, and stormwater infrastructure) protect nearby property values.
- Designed and executed causal econometric research on how climate hazards affect housing markets, vulnerability, and market repricing, with implications for property valuation and insurance.
- Examined property insurance under rising catastrophe risk as research complementing the housing-market work, including how flood-zone designation relates to property-market outcomes.
- Built analysis-ready panels in R, joining geospatial layers (land use, land cover, and tree cover) to parcel-level property records at scale using ArcGIS and QGIS.
- Co-authored a peer-reviewed article using stated-preference econometrics to estimate household willingness to pay for improved hurricane forecasting.
